A **block** is a template for one part of a page: a photo beside some text, a row of
cards, a closing banner. The block brings the layout and the look; you fill in its words
and photos. A page’s **Body** is a stack of blocks, one after another, so whatever you build
stays in keeping with the rest of the site.

Add one with the **+** on the Body field; it lands at the bottom, so drag it up into place.
To see what each one looks like, browse the [Block gallery](./block-gallery.md).

## Pick by what you have

Start at the top and take the first match.

| If what you have is…                               | Reach for                                                 |

| One sentence someone said, worth its own space     | [Quote](./block-gallery.md#quote)                         |
| Three or four parallel things, each a line or two  | [Text cards](./block-gallery.md#text-cards)               |

## Rules of thumb

- **Order matters → Roadmap. Order doesn’t → Key points.** They look alike, but numbering
  things that aren’t a sequence tells the reader something untrue about how to read them.
- **Cards are for parallel things.** If your three cards aren’t the same _kind_ of thing,
  they should be prose.
- **Callout or Note with logo?** A Callout is a heading over text with a green accent rule,
  for one point that must land. A Note with logo is a compact tinted panel with room for a
  logo beside the text, and no heading.
- **One Closing banner per page, and it goes last.** It’s the loudest block, and it closes
  flush against the footer. The form won’t save a page with two, or with one that isn’t last.
- **Two Photo beside text blocks in a row need different backgrounds**, or they read as one
  long block.

## A page, block by block

The [Plan a Visit](/visit/) page is the one a first-time guest actually reads. Here is why
each block is the one it is.

| Block                 | On the page                               | Why this one                                                                                             |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| _(top of page)_       | “Plan a Visit” and a reassuring intro     | The intro does the emotional work before any logistics.                                                  |
| **Text**              | “Sundays at 10:00am”: when and where      | Facts someone may be scanning for. Prose, not a card: they need to be _read_, and cards invite skimming. |
| **Photo**             | The building, captioned                   | “What am I looking for when I arrive?” A caption can say the thing a photo can’t.                        |
| **Photo beside text** | “What happens on a Sunday”                | Show and tell: the description is more believable next to a photo of it happening.                       |
| **Callout**           | “Will I stand out or be put on the spot?” | The biggest fear, answered where it can’t be skimmed past. That’s what a Callout is for.                 |
| **Photo beside text** | “We make Sundays smooth for parents”      | Same pattern, second audience. White, then Warm sand, keeps the two from reading as one long block.      |
| **Text**              | “What should I wear?”                     | A short practical answer. It doesn’t need a photo or a box.                                              |
| **Photo gallery**     | Three photos, no words                    | Breathing room before the close, so the last impression is faces rather than logistics.                  |
| **Closing banner**    | “A place to belong” and the button        | One action, at the end, on a dark band so it reads as the page’s conclusion.                             |

The shape underneath: **reassure → orient → show → answer the fear → show again → practical
detail → breathe → invite.** Most pages for guests want roughly that arc. You don’t have to
follow it, but if a page feels flat, compare it with this one.
