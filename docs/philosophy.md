# Site Philosophy & Guardrails

Why this site exists and the principles that keep it coherent. Read this before
adding or restructuring pages. For how we _write_, see [voice.md](./voice.md); for how
it _looks_, see [design-system.md](./design-system.md).

---

## The shift: from broadcast to belonging

The old site was built on a **broadcast** model — _here's everything we do, come find
us._ This site is built on a **belonging** model — _here's what life here feels like,
and where you might fit._

That isn't cosmetic. Every page is in service of a single question a first-time visitor
is actually asking: **"Could this be a place for me and my family?"**

The primary audience is **people outside the church** — young families and first-time
guests — not the people already here. Insiders can navigate any site; newcomers can't.
So we optimize for the newcomer, on purpose.

---

## The core idea: a filter, not a persuasion site

We are **not** trying to convince everyone to come. We're helping the **right people
recognize themselves** — and helping others self-select out, with no hard feelings.

This gives us one ruthless test for any sentence:

> **If it could describe any church, it's too generic to keep.**

Generic copy ("a welcoming community on a journey") reassures insiders and tells a
newcomer nothing. Specific, human signals do the real work: _"Sundays at 10am,"_
_"K–5 students stay in the service one Sunday a month,"_ _"all volunteers are
background-checked."_ Concrete beats warm-and-vague every time.

## The guardrails

These are the worldview tenets every contributor must hold. (Word-choice rules that
flow from them live in [voice.md](./voice.md).)

### 1. A filtering site, not a persuasion site

Help the right people recognize themselves. **Guardrail:** if a sentence could apply to
any church, it is too generic — replace it with a specific, human signal.

### 2. Avoid "program gravity"

Resist collapsing into a list of programs. **Pattern:** group content around **life
situations**, not church departments or offerings. Avoid "Other things we offer"
sections.

### 3. Intentional asymmetry

The site is not evenly structured by design. **Rule:** don't "balance" the site for
symmetry. Add pages only when there is real content and a clear user need. Quality and
readiness trump theoretical completeness.

### 4. The "Doors" model

A worldview shift from "What we do" to **"How we show up in people's lives."** A newcomer
doesn't search for "the Care Ministry" — they're having a hard week and wondering if
anyone would help. Lead with the situation, then offer a specific door. (The vocabulary
this implies is in [voice.md](./voice.md).)

### 5. Implicit "no pressure"

Low pressure is communicated through **structure and clarity**, not by repeating "no
pressure" everywhere. **Rule:** use explicit reassurance only where there is real
perceived risk (e.g. care situations).

### 6. Protect "time" clarity

Narrative pages stay evergreen. **Rule:** anything that needs frequent updating belongs
in a dedicated module or component (e.g. the Calendar / What's Happening), never baked
into narrative copy that should stay true for years.

### 7. Photos as vignettes, not decoration

Photos are glimpses of real life, not stock imagery or posed portraits. **Constraint:**
photos are **not mapped 1:1** to stories — that avoids tokenism and "forced"
representation. (Visual rules in [design-system.md](./design-system.md).)

### 8. Emotional sequencing

Pages work in a deliberate order (see below). **Implication:** don't optimize pages in
isolation; consider the user's emotional state on arrival.

### 9. Strategically incomplete

The site is a **high-signal front door**, not an exhaustive catalog of everything the
church does. Incompleteness is a strategic choice — a clear, confident site beats a
complete-but-cluttered one.

### 10. Internal consistency

Families, Youth, and Care pages must "sound like the same place." Match tone, structure,
and pacing across sections — and use the shared design tokens and components so they
look like the same place too.

## Emotional sequencing

Pages are designed to move a visitor through a sequence, not to stand alone:

1. **Home** → _"I might belong here."_
2. **I'm New / Families / Youth** → _"I understand what this would feel like."_
3. **For Our Neighbors / Next Steps** → _"I can engage at my own pace."_

---

## Information architecture

The site is a **three-stage funnel**, plus a parallel pathway for the wider community.
It is intentionally asymmetric — pages exist only where there's real content and need.

### Primary navigation

1. **I'm New** — the front door; answers the emotional and practical questions a guest has.
2. **About** — who we are, what we believe, leadership, ethos.
3. **Families** — kids, youth, and family life.
4. **For Our Neighbors** — organized around **needs, not departments** (the Doors model).
5. **What's Happening / Messages** — current events and the sermon archive.

> **Note:** "Next Steps" is removed from primary navigation while its content is still
> being defined. The page exists at `/next-steps/` but is not linked from the header.

---

## What we ask of contributors

1. **Stay specific and human.** If a sentence could apply to any church, rewrite it.
2. **Use the system.** Reach for existing tokens and components before inventing new CSS.
3. **Match the voice across sections.** Families, Youth, and Care should sound like the
   same place — because they are.
