# Editorial Voice & Word Choices

How we write. This is the contributor reference for tone and language; the _why_ behind
it lives in [philosophy.md](./philosophy.md). When editing copy, this page and the
filter test ("if it could describe any church, rewrite it") are the two things to keep
open.

---

## The voice in one line

**Grounded, human, and understated — warm, but not soft.**

We strip out marketing fluff and church insider-speak, because both erode trust with the
exact people we're trying to reach: young families and first-time guests who may have no
church background.

### Calibrating the tone

| Failure mode    | Example                                                             | Why it fails                                  |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| **Too soft**    | "We would love to invite you on a journey…"                         | Says nothing; reassures only insiders.        |
| **Too hard**    | Transactional or instructional commands.                            | Reads as a sign-up funnel, not a welcome.     |
| **Too churchy** | "Fellowship," "discipleship," "plug in."                            | Assumes you're already on the inside.         |
| **Aim for**     | "Visiting a church can feel awkward. Here's what actually happens." | Grounded, specific, and slightly understated. |

## Words we avoid, and what we prefer

| We avoid                                                        | Because                               | We prefer                                                                         |
| --------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "Fellowship," "discipleship," "plug in," "outreach," "ministry" | Jargon that assumes you're already in | "Connection," "support," "find a place," "be part of," "learning to follow Jesus" |
| "We'd love to invite you on a journey…"                         | Too soft; says nothing                | "Visiting a church can feel awkward. Here's what actually happens."               |
| "Other ministries we offer…"                                    | Program gravity — a list nobody reads | Group content around real life situations                                         |
| Repeating "no pressure" everywhere                              | Protesting too much                   | Communicate low pressure through _structure and clarity_                          |
| In/out or "us vs. them" language in nav                         | Sorts people before they've arrived   | Needs-based, situation-first labels                                               |

The terminology rule is the language side of the **Doors model** (see
[philosophy.md](./philosophy.md)): organize around _how the church shows up in people's
lives_, not internal departments. Avoid churchy jargon and in/out language; don't assume
the reader's theology — but equally, don't shy from being distinctive and naming unique
values.

---

## Situation-first writing

Start with the reader's emotional or practical reality, then offer a specific way we show
up.

> **Pattern:** Describe a human situation → offer a specific door.

- ✅ _"When life is overwhelming, even simple tasks can feel heavy."_ (then: Meals of Care)
- ❌ _"We have a meals ministry."_

---

## Two non-negotiables that keep us honest

- **Belief-neutral, but not diluted.** Don't assume the reader is Christian or familiar
  with church. Never require agreement to belong. But also never hide our convictions or
  water them down. Welcoming and distinctive at the same time.
- **Evergreen copy.** Anything that changes often (event times, schedules) lives in a
  dedicated module like What's On — never baked into narrative
  pages that should stay true for years.

---

## A worked example

The test for every sentence is the filter test. Watch it in action:

- ❌ "We're a welcoming community on a journey of faith together." → could be any church.
- ✅ "Sundays at 10am. Kids stay in the service one Sunday a month, and there's coffee
  before and after." → only true of _this_ place, and a newcomer can picture it.

If you can't picture a specific person recognizing themselves in a sentence, it isn't
doing its job yet.
