<Section eyebrow="How it began" heading="It started with a friendship">

In 1999, Christopher Sure — a high-school teacher and lay pastor from Kodera, in western Kenya — was offered a place at the Lutheran Bible Institute in Issaquah. He and his wife, Florence, had been praying for exactly that for five years. So even with three young children at home, they decided he had to go.

Pine Lake became Christopher’s church while he studied, and then something more than that: the congregation covered his schooling and flew him home each summer and Christmas so the work he’d started in Kodera wouldn’t stall while he was away. That first Christmas, a small team from Pine Lake flew out to see the town for themselves. They came back convinced this was a friendship worth keeping for the long haul.

</Section>

<Callout heading="Six kilometers, each way">

That’s how far some students walk — a few of them run — to get to school each morning. For their families, it’s worth it: two meals a day, good teachers, and a real shot at high school and beyond.

</Callout>

<Section eyebrow="School life" heading="Not just the classroom">

The kids love school, and not only for the lessons. PLA runs a full slate of sports and scouting, and in 2019 its girls’ volleyball team went all the way to the national tournament and placed second in Kenya.

</Section>

<Section eyebrow="What comes after" heading="Graduates who keep going">

Kenyan eighth-graders sit a national exam that determines where — and whether — they go on to high school. PLA students consistently place well, and nearly every graduate has gone on to finish high school. In 2022 the school hit a milestone it had been waiting sixteen years for: the first PLA alumni to graduate from college came back to speak at graduation day.

Maybe the biggest change is in the parents. Years ago, many in Kodera assumed their kids did poorly on the exams because they weren’t smart. Nobody believes that anymore.

</Section>

<Section eyebrow="The wider partnership" heading="More than a school">

The school is the largest piece of a partnership that has touched most corners of life in Kodera. Alongside it, Pine Lake has funded a three-room medical clinic in Karabach — St. John’s, run by Kenyan nurses — helped start a certified seed and fertilizer bank for local farmers, sent medical teams, and bought the fifty-foot tent that doubled as community center and home for a congregation of two hundred.

Christopher has never held elected office in Kodera, but the town looks to him as its leader all the same. He and Florence have been married more than thirty years; their three children are grown now, and there are two grandchildren.

</Section>

<Section eyebrow="The partnership today" heading="How Pine Lake supports the school">

Our support is direct and personal. Gifts from Pine Lake go to the school itself and to Christopher as its principal, rather than through a large agency. Over the years, people from Pine Lake have traveled to Kodera to spend time at the school. It’s less a project than a long friendship between two communities.

</Section>
