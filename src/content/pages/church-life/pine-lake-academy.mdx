<Section eyebrow="How it began" heading="It started with a friendship">

In 1999, Christopher Sure — a high-school teacher and pastor from Kodera, a town in western Kenya — came to Issaquah to study at the Lutheran Bible Institute. Friends brought him to a service at Pine Lake, and he quickly became part of the community. The church helped fund his studies, and that Christmas a small team flew to Kodera to meet the town he’d been telling everyone about. They came home convinced this was a friendship worth keeping for the long haul.

</Section>

<Callout heading="Six kilometers, each way">

That’s how far some students walk — a few run it — to get to school each morning. Families in Kodera want their kids at PLA because of what happens inside: teachers who know every child, two meals a day, and classrooms where kids genuinely learn.

</Callout>

<Section eyebrow="What comes after" heading="Graduates who keep going">

Kenyan eighth-graders sit a national exam that determines where — and whether — they go on to high school. PLA students consistently place well, and nearly every graduate has gone on to finish high school. In 2022 the school hit a milestone it had been waiting sixteen years for: the first PLA alumni to graduate from college came back to speak at graduation day.

</Section>

<Section eyebrow="The partnership today" heading="How Pine Lake supports the school">

Our support is direct and personal. Gifts from Pine Lake go to the school itself and to its principal — Christopher Sure, who still leads PLA today — rather than through a large agency. Over the years, people from Pine Lake have traveled to Kodera to spend time at the school, and Christopher still visits Sammamish when he’s in the U.S. It’s less a project than a long friendship between two communities.

</Section>
